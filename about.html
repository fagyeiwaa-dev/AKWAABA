A country of stories and strength

About Ghana

Ghana sits on the Gulf of Guinea in West Africa and is known for its welcoming communities, resilient history, and diverse natural beauty.

A quick introduction

Discover Ghana at a glance

From bustling cities to forested hills and coastal towns, Ghana offers a mix of historical depth, cultural creativity, and vibrant everyday life.

Location

Ghana is in West Africa, bordered by Côte d'Ivoire to the west, Burkina Faso to the north, and Togo to the east.

Capital city

Accra is the national capital and a major center for trade, culture, education, and government.

Regions

Ghana is divided into 16 administrative regions, each with its own local traditions and landscapes.

History and identity

Ghana's story in context

Colonial history

Before independence, Ghana was part of the Gold Coast colony. Its historical role in trade, migration, and imperial politics shaped much of its modern identity.

Independence

Ghana became the first sub-Saharan African country to gain independence from colonial rule in 1957, a landmark moment in African history.

People and language

Ghana is home to many ethnic groups and languages. English is the official language, while many Ghanaian languages are spoken in daily life.

Culture and values

Values such as hospitality, community, and respect for elders remain important across many Ghanaian communities.

Places and landscapes

From coast to highlands

Ghana's landscape ranges from sandy beaches and lagoons to rainforests, mountains, and savannah regions.

Coastal Ghana

Long stretches of coastline feature beaches, fishing communities, forts, and coastal towns rich in history.

Forests and parks

Protected areas such as Kakum and Mole offer a glimpse into Ghana's biodiversity and natural heritage.

Volta and rivers

The Volta River and Lake Volta shape life, energy, and transportation in parts of the country.

Traditional communities

Many towns and villages preserve important cultural practices, architecture, and local leadership systems.

Leadership and governance

Government and national leadership

President John Dramani Mahama

Ghana is a constitutional democracy led by the President, who serves as Head of State and Head of Government. The executive branch works with Parliament and the judiciary to govern the country.

How government works

Ghana's democratic system is built on the rule of law, a multi-party political system, and public institutions that are meant to serve all citizens. Parliament makes laws, the executive implements policy, and the courts help uphold constitutional governance.

Strong institutions

Ghana is known for its stable democratic traditions, active public institutions, and a strong emphasis on civic participation.

Natural resources

Ghana is rich in minerals, including gold, diamond, manganese, and bauxite, all of which contribute to the country's economy.

Economic value

Mining and resource extraction remain important parts of Ghana's economy, alongside agriculture, trade, energy, and services.
